Purpose: To obtain a generalized method to convert to and from raw and central moments (i.e., changing origin arbitrarily).
Why useful:

- Eradicates the need for memorizing complex formula
- General: Works for converting any origin to any other origin
- Easier (once got hold of)
- Works for moment of any order
- Also works for named distributions

Steps

- Show the existing method
- Derive the new method
- Check for arbitrary moments
- Check for a few named distributions (discrete: Binomial, continuous: Normal)
- Write R functions for benchmark with existing method

# Strength

  ### 1. Value & Significance of the Topic                                                
                                                                                          
  Yes, it is a strong, highly practical, and elegant topic.                               
                                                                                          
  While the binomial theorem itself is fundamental, the value of this paper lies in       
  unification, pedagogical clarity, and computational vectorization:                      
                                                                                          
  1. Solving a Major Pedagogical Pain Point:                                              
  In standard statistics courses and textbooks (e.g., Kendall & Stuart, Mood et al.,      
  Walpole), moment transformations are introduced as a fragmented set of disconnected,    
  order-specific formulas ($\mu_2 = \mu'_2 - \mu'_1^2$, $\mu_3 = \mu'_3 - 3\mu'_2\mu'_1 + 
  2\mu'_1^3$, etc.). Students and practitioners view them as formulas to memorize rather  
  than applications of a single principle.                                                
  2. The "Symbolic Binomial Trick" is Intuitive and Memorable:                            
  Expressing any moment transformation as (a + b)ʳ with the simple symbolic substitution  
  $a^j \mapsto \mu'_j(a)$ allows anyone who knows Pascal's triangle to reconstruct        
  conversion formulas of any order r in seconds without opening a textbook.               
  3. Universal Scope:                                                                     
  Instead of treating Raw-to-Raw, Raw-to-Central, and Central-to-Raw as distinct subjects,
  a single origin shift parameter b = a - k handles all three seamlessly across empirical 
  data, PMFs, PDFs, and theoretical distributions.                                        
  4. Computational Efficiency (O(r²) vs. O(n·r)):                                         
  In data analysis and streaming applications, recalculating moments from scratch over a  
  sample of size n after an origin shift requires O(n·r) operations. Transforming pre-    
  computed moments via the lower-triangular binomial matrix

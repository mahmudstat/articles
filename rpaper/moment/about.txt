Purpose: To obtain a generalized method to convert to and from raw and central moments (i.e., changing origin arbitrarily).
Why useful:

- Eradicates the need for memorizing complex formula
- General: Works for converting any origin to any other origin
- Easier (once got hold of)
- Works for moment of any order
- Also works for named distributions

Steps

- Show the existing method
- Derive the new method
- Check for arbitrary moments
- Check for a few named distributions (discrete: Binomial, continuous: Normal)
- Write R functions for benchmark with existing method

# Framing (revised: pedagogical paper for JSDSE)

The claim is pedagogical, not mathematical. The origin-shift identity is
classical (Kendall & Stuart 1977) -- the paper says so explicitly. The
contribution is: the (a+b)^r mnemonic, the reduction to one parameter
b = a - k, and the convmoment package.

Claim scope: all levels (argued structurally -- the rule needs only the
binomial theorem + linearity, so it works from secondary upward).
Evidence scope: higher-secondary (classes 11-12), Statistics 1st paper,
chapter on Moments, Skewness and Kurtosis. Presented as the *hardest*
audience, not the only one.

[1] PRIORITY -- read this one first
    Mocko, M., Wagler, A. E., Lesser, L. M., Francis, W. S., Blush, J. M.,
    Schleicher, K. & Barrientos, P. S. (2024).
    "What They Remember May Not Be What They Understand: A Study of Mnemonic
    Recall and Performance by Introductory Statistics Students."
    Journal of Statistics and Data Science Education 32(4):416-431.
    https://doi.org/10.1080/26939169.2024.2334905

    n=1323, 14 mnemonics. Acronym-type mnemonics were RECALLED more often, but
    longer phrase-type mnemonics were EXPLAINED and APPLIED more often. They
    recommend scaffolding students from recalling a mnemonic to using one.

    Why it matters: cuts against a naive version of our claim (remembering a
    device is not understanding it) -- engaging with that is what makes the
    paper look literate rather than promotional. But it also SUPPORTS our
    design: ours is not an acronym to recall but a procedure to execute, i.e.
    the elaborated end of their spectrum where application was strongest.
    Target section: 7.1 (What the Approach Achieves).

[2] Mocko, M., Lesser, L. M., Wagler, A. E. & Francis, W. S. (2017).

    An actual effectiveness assessment. Use as a design template for the study
